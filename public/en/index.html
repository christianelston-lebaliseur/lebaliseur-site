For local searches, Google shows a map with three businesses. Most customers stop there. The others exist, but nobody sees them.

And search is changing: more and more people simply ask an AI (ChatGPT, Gemini, Google's AI Mode). It names two or three businesses, picked from the same signals: listings, reviews, directories and a clear website.

What keeps you out is often simple: an incomplete Google profile, wrong opening hours, few recent reviews, or a website that's slow or hard to read on a phone.

Chris Elston, le baliseur

For more than twenty years I've worked with tour operators, moving travellers between Geneva and Grenoble airports and the Alpine resorts: up to 10,000 people a week in season. I led the growth and development of the business there, building long-term relationships with tour operators. I know what makes a business grow, and what holds it back.

Before that, in England, I started out in marketing: I planned and sold advertising campaigns in the business press, for clients ranging from small business owners to the managing directors of groups like Hewlett Packard and Nissan.

On the digital side, I have a diploma in information technology: I've built websites and apps and run their marketing. I've worked with Google for 26 years, I know SEO inside out, and I use AI tools every day to work fast without cutting corners. I also handle visual design.

Today I'm putting all of that experience to work for small businesses and independents: my understanding of hospitality, of service businesses, and of how a business develops and grows. The aim is simple: to help you succeed, with clear steps, deadlines met and results measured.

I live in Annecy-le-Vieux and work in English and French.
